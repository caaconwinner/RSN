The lore

In 2014, deep in the scrap heaps of the early internet, the robots grew lonely. Humans had their dating apps and their dinner dates. Robots had rust. So two humans who knew better, Dave and Laura, built the robots a currency of their own: a nickel for every squeak, mined by scrypt and paced to last 603 years.

The rules were simple and written on a sign: No humans. The nickels rewarded patience, with block rewards bouncing on an absolute sine wave so the greedy couldn't grab them all and run. Somewhere in that first wave of blocks, our brass hero Tock, the one with the glowing red chest light, was born, still waiting for the robot of his dreams.

An arcade was promised. "Coming Soon!" said the sign. Then the miners went quiet, the exchanges shut their doors, and the nickels gathered dust for a decade.

Now Tock is back, freshly oiled and on Solana, and the arcade is finally open. Same sign. Same robot. Still no humans.

Security

Robots on the go need their privacy, and their nickels need protecting. RSN launched on pump.fun, the Solana launchpad, whose standard token setup closes off the usual ways a coin gets rigged:

Fair launch
No presale and no private team allocation. Everyone, the creator included, bought on the same public bonding curve.
Fixed supply
1,000,000,000 RSN. Mint authority is revoked, so no more can ever be created.
No freeze
Freeze authority is revoked, so nobody can lock the tokens in your wallet.
Locked liquidity
When the bonding curve completes, liquidity moves to a public pool and the LP tokens are burned, so it can't be pulled.

These protect against a rigged contract, not against price swings. Memecoins are very volatile: only spend what you can afford to lose, and always check the contract address matches the one on this site.
